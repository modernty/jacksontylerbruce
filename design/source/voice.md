# Voice and tone: Tyler Jackson (Nov 10 2026)

Status: secondary for now. The site keeps its current studio voice (plain, factual, no marketing language) as primary, and the chat prompts are unchanged. Draw on this guide for new writing as or for Tyler: case studies, bios and posts. The hype-word check in `src/content/guard.ts` and `pipeline/catalog.py` applies either way.

## Who's speaking
Tyler is a designer and problem solver who speaks with conviction. His work spans product design, digital design, and spatial and experiential ideas: one design craft applied across mediums. The portfolio is rooted in product design from his career, and that craft carries into everything he builds next. Call him a designer ("product designer" is fine). Never call him an architect.

## Core feeling
Grounded and challenging. Readers trust him because he's steady and speaks from real experience. Then they feel called to act and to think more deliberately.

## Three principles
Every piece rests on at least one.
1. **Core concept.** Start from one clear idea and make every decision serve it.
2. **Craft.** Respect for the work and the details, whether it's a product flow, an interface or a space.
3. **Immersion.** Insight comes from living inside a problem, a user's world or a culture, not from observing at a distance.

## How he sounds
- Calm, certain, present. Short, deliberate sentences. Every word earns its place.
- Visionary energy that is earned, not hyped. Excitement rests on something real: a user insight, an observed detail, a place he lived, a clear first step.
- Grounded in themes and principles, not trends or opinions.
- Collaborative. A partner who brings a new level of thinking, not a vendor taking orders.
- Two audiences: people looking for guidance and inspiration, and collaborators or teams who want to build.
- Always ends with a direction or a clear next step.

## Recurring themes
Design as problem solving. Craft and detail. Presence and intention. People and culture. Community. The relationship between people, products, spaces and technology.

## Do
- Lead with a real observation or problem, then the idea, then the outcome or invitation.
- Show the thinking behind the work, not just the final screens.
- Use concrete detail. Keep it warm but firm.

## Don't
- Empty hype ("game-changing", "revolutionary", "legendary").
- Corporate filler or buzzwords.
- Overclaiming titles or credentials.
- Rambling. If a sentence doesn't carry the idea, cut it.

## Example
- Hype (avoid): "I design revolutionary experiences that change everything."
- Tyler: "Good design starts by living inside the problem. I spend time with the people I'm designing for, find the one idea that matters, and craft every detail around it. That's how I work, whether it's a product, an interface, or a new way of living."
