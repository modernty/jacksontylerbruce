Question 02 · Loyalty scan test · Earning first use

How can we turn in-store behaviors into an introduction to the agent?

Prototype readyFraming test · Optimum members, in and out of SDM

The hypothesis

The checkout scan is the one second Loblaw already has permission to speak. An introduction framed as something the card just unlocked lands as a perk. The identical message framed as a feature announcement lands as an ad and burns the moment. Same agent, opposite first impression, decided by the frame.

What we’re testing

The Optimum scan at checkout as the acquisition moment. Habits people already have in store, turned into first conversations instead of campaigns.

Why we’re asking

Ninety percent of Canadians live within ten kilometres of a store, and acquisition is currently imagined as campaigns. If the scan works, the first conversation costs nothing per person and happens at the exact moment of proven loyalty. First frames are also close to impossible to re run. Whatever this moment says, it says once.

The prototype

  1. 1The Optimum card scans at self checkout.
  2. 2A notification arrives framed as something the card unlocked.
  3. 3One tap opens the agent mid errand.
  4. 4The greeting already knows the points balance and the pharmacy.

How we’d run it

Two framings of the same moment, traded off by every person. The perk frame speaks in reward language and points context. The feature frame announces an assistant. Everyone sees both at a simulated checkout, in random order.

An intercept read seconds after a real scan, because a lab reaction to a mockup and a real reaction at a real register are different data. We measure open intent and the creepy read right there.

The decline path, tested on purpose. What a no thanks does next, whether it stays quiet or asks again, and how much resentment a re ask earns.

What this settles, stated plainly. The winning frame and the moment. It does not settle national opt in rates, which need a live pilot. If the frame result warrants it, we will say that is the next study rather than dress this one up as the answer.

On the card

Ninety percent of Canadians live within ten kilometres of a store, and acquisition is still imagined as campaigns. The Optimum scan becomes the first conversation, and one tap opens an agent that already knows the points balance and the pharmacy.
